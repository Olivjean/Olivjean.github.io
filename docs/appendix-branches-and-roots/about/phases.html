Douglas Araujo & Jean de Oliveira

Phases of the land-demarcation administrative process

Brazil's Federal Constitution (Art. 231) recognises the Indigenous peoples' original rights over the lands they traditionally occupy. The process by which the Union (Federal Government) formally demarcates these lands runs through five administrative phases, regulated by Decree 1.775/1996. FUNAI (Fundação Nacional dos Povos Indígenas) conducts the technical work. The Ministry of Justice and the Presidency issue the binding legal acts.

The five phases are cumulative. Each new act adds a layer of protection on top of the earlier ones, so a land at Registered carries all the safeguards introduced at Declared and Ratified. A small set of lands follows other tracks: a reserve under Law 6.001/1973, a FUNAI use-restriction order for isolated peoples, or ownership by the community itself. These are described under Other tracks below.

Modern pipeline (Decree 1.775/1996)
1Under study em estudo A multidisciplinary working group, formally instituted by a FUNAI Portaria, conducts anthropological, historical, land-tenure, cartographic and environmental studies to characterise the territory traditionally occupied by the Indigenous group.
2Delimited delimitada The studies are concluded and approved by the Presidency of FUNAI, and the land's boundaries are published in a Despacho do Presidente da FUNAI in the Federal Gazette (DOU). At this stage the area is formally delineated. It is not yet legally protected against third parties.
3Declared declarada The administrative process is forwarded to the Minister of Justice, who issues a Portaria Declaratória recognising the area as an Indigenous Land and authorising the physical demarcation: placement of boundary markers and georeferencing. The paper counts a land as legally protected from this phase on.
4Ratified homologada A Presidential Decree ratifies the demarcation and publishes the materialised and georeferenced limits of the Indigenous Land. It adds a further layer of protection on top of the declaratory order.
5Registered regularizada FUNAI assists the Secretariat of the Federal Patrimony (SPU) in registering the ratified land in the local real-estate registry (CRI) and in the SPU's records, formalising Union ownership with Indigenous usufruct rights.
Other tracks
Reserve reserva indígena A decree creates an Indigenous Reserve (Reserva Indígena, under Art. 27 of Law 6.001/1973, the Indigenous Statute). The land is acquired through direct purchase, expropriation or donation. Indigenous Reserves follow a separate legal path from the five-phase pipeline above. Some reserves were created before the 1988 Constitution, typically by decree of the Serviço de Proteção aos Índios (SPI, FUNAI's predecessor) or by mid-20th-century Presidential Decree. Many of these areas have since been re-recognised through the modern demarcation pipeline.
Use restriction restrição de uso When evidence points to the presence of isolated Indigenous peoples in an area whose demarcation has not yet been completed, FUNAI may establish, by its own order under Art. 7 of Decree 1.775/1996, a temporary prohibition on third-party entry and on activities such as logging, mining and farming.
Community-owned dominial indígena Lands owned by Indigenous communities themselves (not by the Union), held with full title. These follow ordinary civil-law property registration and are not subject to FUNAI's demarcation process.

Legal status on the cards

Each card shows the land's legal status on 31 December 2024, the last day of the paper's panel. It follows the paper's classification (Appendix A). The status is the most advanced of the five phases above that the land had reached by that date. A land outside that pipeline shows its track instead: Reserve, Use restriction or Community-owned. A land with none of these shows Not yet in process.

What “legally protected” means in the paper

A land counts as legally protected in a given year once it has reached the Declared phase, or the equivalent step on one of the other tracks: a decree creating a Reserve, a use restriction, or formal recognition as community-owned land. The paper uses Declared as the threshold because it is the first government act that bars outsiders from using the land for economic activity. A land at Under study or Delimited still counts as protected when one of the other tracks also covers it.
